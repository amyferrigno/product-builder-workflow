# Product Planning - Oct 3, 2026

## Attendees
PM, Product Marketing Lead, Sales Lead

---

PM: Alright, let's finalize scope for the search feature. Users want better ways to find past meetings.

Sales Lead: Absolutely. We have a big deal pending with a large prospect. They specifically asked for search. I promised them it would work across all of their plan levels—they use our Standard and Enterprise plans depending on the department.

PM: Search is complex to implement across plan boundaries. I was thinking we launch it for Enterprise only first, to keep it simple and to give them a reason to upgrade.

Product Marketing Lead: That's actually good positioning. We can market search as an Enterprise differentiator. That helps our pricing story.

Sales Lead: No, no, no. I told them it's on all plans. If we restrict it to Enterprise, I lose the deal. They'll see it as a bait-and-switch.

PM: I didn't know you'd already committed to that. This is a significant scope difference.

Sales Lead: I had to commit. They were evaluating competitors. Search on all plans is table stakes for them.

Product Marketing Lead: But if we launch it on all plans, we lose the upsell momentum. Enterprise customers won't see it as a premium feature.

PM: So we have two conflicting requirements: Sales needs it on all plans to keep the deal, but Product Marketing wants it as Enterprise-only to support premium positioning.

Sales Lead: The deal with the large prospect is worth $200K annually. We can't lose it over this.

Product Marketing Lead: I understand. But if we devalue search by putting it on Standard, we lose upsell leverage for other deals.

PM: This is exactly the kind of decision we need to make together, not in silos. I'm not committing to either approach right now. Let's document this conflict and get leadership to decide.

Sales Lead: Fair, but please decide fast. The prospect needs an answer by end of week.
